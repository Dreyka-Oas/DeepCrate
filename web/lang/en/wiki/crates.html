The six crates

Six tiers, each nine slots larger than the one before, and each crafted by surrounding the previous crate with the material that names the next.

The order follows how much trouble the material is to reach, which is why breeze and echo sit at the top: one drops in a trial chamber, the other on the floor of an ancient city.

Two crates that pair up

Two crates of the same tier placed side by side open one screen, the way vanilla chests do. The slots of both halves run on without a break, sorting treats them as one series, and the two share a single module: what sits on one half's tab counts for the whole pair.

A pair of echo crates is therefore 144 slots, and 432 with sixteen row modules. Two different tiers side by side stay two separate crates.

What a crate keeps of its name

A crate renamed on an anvil keeps that name once placed, and the screen shows it in the top left. A name wider than the room available is cut before the page number rather than drawn over it.

Breaking a full crate. The contents drop on the floor cut into stacks of 64, because that is all a ground stack can carry. A double echo crate with a 512 module therefore returns over a thousand stacks at once, and nobody picks that up before it despawns. Empty it before you break it.
